# M6: the art pass

**Goal (PLAN §8):** screenshots read as the target style. **Style (PLAN §6, confirmed 2026-11-18):**
shapes and surfaces drawn like vector art (flat colour regions, crisp edges, bold graphic patterns,
no photographic noise), lit like a modern game (GI, volumetric fog, heavy glow, AgX). Ultrakill's
scale, brutalist-gothic architecture and saturated hellish palettes, without its low-res filtering.

**Decided:** Blender is installed portable in `tools/.blender/` (git-ignored; headless scripts export
`.glb`, so Godot never needs a Blender path). Free assets from the web are fine without asking, each
one logged in `assets/CREDITS.md`, with game-safe licences only (CC0 preferred, CC-BY with credit).
Collectibles are cut from M5.

## Constraints that carry over

- **Performance target:** the owner's Iris Xe at 3072 × 1602, render scale 0.6. Today the Jungle is
  ~15.6 ms of GPU at the defaults, and the Inferno ~29 ms flying over the whole map with the speed overlay.
  Every new effect gets measured, and costly ones get a setting.
- **Generated maps stay generated:** materials are assigned **by role** (`stone`, `basalt`,
  `iron`, `wood`, …, per map in `assets/tres/materials/greybox/<map>/`). Re-skinning a role means
  replacing that `.tres`, not editing scenes. New meshes (props) are placed by the generators too.
- **Collision stays simple** (boxes, terrain); detailed meshes are visual only.
- Headless checks still gate every commit. **Looks can only be judged by the owner in the editor**, so
  each step ends with "please look at X".

## Progress

**Step 1, first pass (2026-11-18), waiting for the owner's look in the editor:**
- `tools/texgen/patterns.py` (Python, PIL + numpy) defines vector patterns as shapes and writes
  each as an SVG (`assets/images/patterns/src/`, to look at) and a packed data map
  (`assets/images/patterns/*.png`: R beveled height, G per-block tone, B accent; lossless +
  mipmaps). Patterns: `ashlar_large`, `ashlar_small`, `flagstone`, `flagstone_plain`, `plates`, `trim`.
- `assets/gdshaders/world_vector.gdshader`: dominant-axis world projection. It turns those maps
  into crisp `fwidth`-thresholded joints and inlays, bevel normals, a highlight on bevel rims,
  per-block tone, emissive accents (ember cracks that breathe), and metal/roughness per region.
- `assets/tres/materials/art/inferno/<role>.tres`: one material per role. `MapGen.art_dir` makes a
  generator use an art material for a role when one exists (Inferno: all roles but `ember`).
  This applies across the whole Inferno, not only the Basilica (roles are shared), but the
  Basilica is the place to judge it.
- **Settings > Lighting:** Low (default) or High. High turns on SDFGI and, where the
  environment opts in (`metadata/volumetric_fog`), volumetric fog. The Inferno does opt in.
- `tools/checks/shaders.gd`: every shader parses (headless runs the shader parser).

## Steps

1. **Art test room (the gate).** One real place, the Inferno's BASILICA OF SMOULDER, done fully:
   - a triplanar world shader (`world_triplanar.gdshader`) with vector-style detail: panel lines,
     bevel highlights, bold patterns from textures generated as SVG (`tools/texgen/`), with
     roughness and normal maps from the same shapes;
   - its colour grade, volumetric fog, glow, and GI (SDFGI vs VoxelGI, measured);
   - the lava's glow lighting the hall.

   The owner judges the look and the cost, and we iterate until they approve. Nothing else is
   mass-produced before that.
2. **Material sets per map:** the approved shader applied to every role on the Inferno, then the
   Jungle (teal/green; weathered limestone, dark wood) and the Overworld (warm gold; grass and rock
   terrain, sandstone ruins). Terrain3D gets textured layers to match. The Gym stays greybox (it's a
   measuring tool).
3. **Characters:** a humanoid (made in Blender, or a free rigged pack retargeted to one skeleton)
   with animations: run, slide, jump, fall, wall-slide, dash, slam, frozen, stunned. An AnimationTree
   is driven by the `Character` state and signals. The Torch's fire sits on the model. First person
   still hides your own body (render layer 2).
4. **Environment props** (Blender or packs): braziers, chains, pews, tombs, trees and foliage
   (MultiMesh with visibility ranges), rocks, ruins. Each one swaps in for a greybox box where it
   reads better, keeping the box collision.
5. **VFX:** tag flash, dash trails, ember trails and scorch marks behind the Torch, lava sparks,
   waterfall spray, a retune of the speed overlay.
6. **Performance pass:** occlusion culling between Inferno halls (`OccluderInstance3D`), visibility
   ranges everywhere, per-setting costs re-measured, and new settings for anything optional.

**Done when:** each map has a screenshot the owner says reads as the target style, and the game still
holds its frame budget at the default settings.
