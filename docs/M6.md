# M6: the art pass

**Goal (PLAN §8):** screenshots read as the target style. **Style (PLAN §6, confirmed 2026-11-18):**
shapes and surfaces drawn like vector art (flat colour regions, crisp edges, bold graphic patterns,
no photographic noise), lit like a modern game (GI, volumetric fog, heavy glow, AgX). Ultrakill's
scale, brutalist-gothic architecture and saturated hellish palettes, without its low-res filtering.

**Decided:** Blender is installed portable in `tools/.blender/` (git-ignored; headless scripts export
`.glb`, so Godot never needs a Blender path). Free assets from the web are fine without asking, each
one logged in `assets/CREDITS.md`, with game-safe licences only (CC0 preferred, CC-BY with credit).
Collectibles are cut from M5.

## Constraints that carry over

- **Performance target:** the owner's Iris Xe at 3072 × 1602, render scale 0.6. Today the Jungle is
  ~15.6 ms of GPU at the defaults, and the Inferno ~29 ms flying over the whole map with the speed overlay.
  Every new effect gets measured, and costly ones get a setting.
- **Generated maps stay generated:** materials are assigned **by role** (`stone`, `basalt`,
  `iron`, `wood`, …, per map in `assets/tres/materials/greybox/<map>/`). Re-skinning a role means
  replacing that `.tres`, not editing scenes. New meshes (props) are placed by the generators too.
- **Collision stays simple** (boxes, terrain); detailed meshes are visual only.
- Headless checks still gate every commit. **Looks can only be judged by the owner in the editor**, so
  each step ends with "please look at X".

## Progress

**Step 1, first pass (2026-11-18), waiting for the owner's look in the editor:**
- `tools/texgen/patterns.py` (Python, PIL + numpy) defines vector patterns as shapes and writes
  each as an SVG (`assets/images/patterns/src/`, to look at) and a packed data map
  (`assets/images/patterns/*.png`: R beveled height, G per-block tone, B accent; lossless +
  mipmaps). Patterns: `ashlar_large`, `ashlar_small`, `brick`, `flagstone`, `flagstone_plain`, `plates`, `trim`.
- Owner feedback 1: the cathedral floor (big repeated glowing inlays) read too harsh. Floors are now
  polished dark brick in running bond (`brick`, roughness 0.3), with no icons and no glowing cracks.
- Owner feedback 2: too perfect, and the same few bricks everywhere. `brick` is now 512 distinct
  hand-laid bricks on an 8 m tile: corners knocked off, edges out of true, rows a little off,
  some sunk, a few cracked. The shader gives every brick its own tone and shine in every copy of
  the tile (`block_variation`, `rough_variation`; the A channel marks bricks that wrap the tile
  edge so both halves match), and big patches of grime (`grime_strength`) that never repeat.
- `tools/checks/gpu_bench.gd`: windowed (renders; only when the owner allows), measures GPU ms per
  setting and feature from fixed cameras against a baseline taken before and after each test.
- `assets/gdshaders/world_vector.gdshader`: dominant-axis world projection. It turns those maps
  into crisp `fwidth`-thresholded joints and inlays, bevel normals, a highlight on bevel rims,
  per-block tone, emissive accents (ember cracks that breathe), and metal/roughness per region.
- `assets/tres/materials/art/inferno/<role>.tres`: one material per role. `MapGen.art_dir` makes a
  generator use an art material for a role when one exists (Inferno: all roles but `ember`).
  This applies across the whole Inferno, not only the Basilica (roles are shared), but the
  Basilica is the place to judge it.
- **Settings > Lighting:** Low (default) or High. High turns on SDFGI and, where the
  environment opts in (`metadata/volumetric_fog`), volumetric fog. The Inferno does opt in.
- `tools/checks/shaders.gd`: every shader parses (headless runs the shader parser).

**Step 2, materials for the Jungle and Overworld (2026-11-19):** every role has an art material
(`assets/tres/materials/art/<map>/`). New patterns: `planks` (boards, nails, grain), `bark`
(tapered vertical ridges), `leaves` (420 overlapping outlined leaves; faces can now carry their
own outline), `facets` (broken rock). The shader gained `grime_color`: the Jungle's grime is
moss (temple, ruins, rock, wood), the Overworld's is dust. Kickers/ramps keep their orange and
hubs their blue, on riveted plates: they read as gameplay. Water, waterfalls and embers keep
their own shaders. Cost over greybox (gpu_bench, art vs greybox): Jungle +0.6 ms (temple view,
forest floor), Overworld ~0 (noise). Terrain3D ground textures are still the greybox ones.

**Jungle trees, take 2 (2026-11-19).** The owner: the vector bark "looks like bricks", the box
canopies "like Roblox assets". Now:
- Real textures (CC0, `assets/CREDITS.md`): ambientCG Bark012 for every trunk and the `bark` role
  (triplanar on the square gameplay trunks, stumps, log bridges); LeafSet024's leaves composed
  into four leafy twig cards (`tools/texgen/foliage.py` -> `assets/images/foliage/twigs.png`).
- Tree models (`tools/blender/trees.py` -> `assets/models/trees/jungle_trees.glb`): four trees
  (tapered, bent trunk with a root flare, 5-7 branches, ~180 twig cards in clusters at the branch
  tips and the top, normals pointing out of the crown) and a crown alone for the treehouses and
  twins. Preview without the game: `tools/blender/preview_trees.py` (CPU render, no window).
- `foliage.gdshader`: alpha cutout, light through from behind, a slow per-tree sway on game_time.
- MapKit.mesh() / MapGen.models: kits place model meshes, drawn as one MultiMesh per model per
  kit. Forest trees keep an unseen trunk box (collision, wall-jumps, the navmesh).
- ~~Cost: 0.1-0.4 ms~~: wrong, the trees weren't drawing at all. A MultiMesh saved by a headless
  generator is empty (the dummy renderer keeps no instance data): every tree sat at the origin,
  zero size. Now `src/map/model_scatter.gd` keeps the transforms in the scene and builds the
  MultiMesh when the map loads; `tools/checks/models.gd` checks every copy has a real place.
- Real cost, then cut: the trees were ~10 ms (5.5-7 of it their shadows). Each model is split
  into `<tree>_wood` (shadows everywhere) and `<tree>_leaves` (no shadow; a shadow-only twin within
  45 m: `MapGen.model_opts`), and the crowns use fewer, bigger cards (~110). Now 5.8-7.2 ms for
  all trees, 2.3-3.5 ms of it shadows; the Jungle 14.8-16.7 ms total at render scale 0.6.
- Wood: real planks (ambientCG Planks012 on the Jungle, Planks023A on the Overworld). Boxes now get
  UVs in metres along each face's longer side (`MapGen._box_into`), so planks run along each deck
  and textures keep their size on any box.

**Jungle, round 3 (2026-11-19, owner feedback: inside-out trees; "trees with platforms" should
look like real very thick trees, thinning a little going up; the river and pools need a shader
and a shape, not blue rectangles):**
- The forest trees' trunks were wound inside out (the tube builder in trees.py): Godot culled the
  outside. Fixed; `tools/checks/models.gd` now checks every bark surface faces out.
- Treehouse and twins trunks (and stumps) are generated thick trunks (`_trunk_model` in
  jungle_gen.gd): 9-13 angular ridges of uneven height twisting up, buttress-root lobes at the
  foot, a root flare, thinning to 80% (treehouses) / 90% (twins) by the top, the real bark
  world-projected. Treehouse trunks are 3 m in radius; the spiral ledges and the deck ring follow
  the taper (they hug the trunk at every height). Collision: octagon steps inside the valleys
  (twins keep their flat chimney faces, so the wall-jump gap is unchanged).
- Water: `water.gdshader` (colour by the real water depth from the depth buffer, bank foam,
  ripples drifting downstream, fresnel). The river is a ribbon down a meandering course (RIVER
  smoothed, bent side to side, width 0.8-1.3x) over a carved bed that never rises downstream,
  1.2 m deep all along; the pools are discs held by their banks; the east pool spills into the
  river without a seam. `tools/checks/water.gd`: water in its banks, the river full.
- Found on the way: the river distance cells were PackedInt32Arrays appended inside a Dictionary
  (values: the appends went to copies), so the river had no channel and trees stood in it.
- Tools: `tools/checks/shot.gd` (in-game screenshots from fixed cameras, windowed),
  `tools/checks/export_obj.gd` + `tools/blender/preview_obj.py` (a map piece rendered in Blender).
- Cost (gpu_bench, Jungle): the trees 4.4-5.1 ms (2.9-3.7 of it shadows), the water 0.65 ms where
  it fills the view; the Jungle 12.2-13.9 ms at render scale 0.6.

**Distant tree shadows (2026-11-19):** the crowns' shadows stopped 45 m away (per 64 m forest
block, so they popped in and out in chunks as you walked). Now each crown also has a solid
stand-in, `<tree>_shadow` (a squashed 20-face lump per leaf cluster, built in trees.py), drawn
only into the shadow map and only past 45 m (`MapGen.model_opts` "shadow_far"); the detailed
card shadows take over closer in. Tree shadows 3.0-4.4 ms (were 2.9-3.7 without any far shadow;
80-face lumps were 5.9-7 ms). `shot.gd` gains HIDE=<suffix> to compare with and without.

**Leaf shadows setting (2026-11-19):** Off / Low (stand-in lumps only) / Medium (cards to the
map's 45 m, the default) / High (cards to 90 m). The generator puts the card twins in group
`shadow_near` and the lumps in `shadow_far` (meta `shadow_distance`); `STG._apply_leaf_shadows`
moves them. Against Medium (gpu_bench, Jungle, slow power state): Off -2.6 / -3.1 / -4.3 ms,
Low -2.3 / -2.6 / -3.7, High +1.4 / +2.8 / +1.8 (plaza / forest floor / river).
`tools/checks/leaf_shadows.gd` checks the levels headless.

**Worn stone (2026-11-19):** the owner: the stones were "so damn perfect everywhere"; tint and
shape changes don't fix that, damage does. `ashlar` (every map's cut stone) now lays each block
with `worn_block`: a hand-dressed outline, corners broken off (jagged) down to a lower fractured
level or right to the joint, bites out of edges, flaked pits, pores, cracks that sometimes fork.
The shader's `broken_shade` darkens and roughens anything at that lower level (0.35 in the stone
materials). The tile doubled (2048 px, tile_size x2) so four times as many different stones pass
before it repeats.

**Overworld de-bricked (2026-11-19):** the owner: "why is 90% of the game assets just bricks";
every structure was a block pattern and the ground still the greybox grid. Rebuilt from
references, not invented: the Pont du Gard (warm honey limestone, joints only hairlines, rain
streaks, paler tops; pale limestone outcrops, dry grass and olive scrub round it), a random-rubble
wall, lime-plastered walls (Wikimedia Commons). Textures are ambientCG CC0 photo sets, prepared by
`tools/texgen/overworld_textures.py` (the ids and download steps are in its header).
- Ground: three photo layers (Rock023 cliffs, Grass004, Ground079S dry earth), painted vertex by
  vertex by `overworld_gen._paint` (`MapGen.paint`, `add_terrain_layers`): rock where steep, bare
  earth in patches, on the canyon floor and worn into the spawn meadow, grass elsewhere (78 / 15 /
  6 % of the arena; `tools/checks/ground_paint.gd`). Shader `assets/gdshaders/terrain_art.gdshader`:
  Terrain3D's lightweight shader plus anti-tiling (a second, turned read swapped in by a broad
  noise, skipped where the noise is fully one way) and golden-olive macro variation.
- Structures: `assets/gdshaders/world_photo.gdshader` (photo sets projected along the dominant
  axis like world_vector; anti-tiling with a per-material turn, PI for courses and strata; tone
  drift; rain streaks; dusty tops; optional faint joints). Aqueduct and ruins: honey limestone
  (Concrete012, faint joints from `ashlar_joints`, a wear-free pattern). Towers: rubble (Rocks001).
  Blocks and mesa pillars: layered limestone (Rock023). Hubs: chipped blue paint (PaintedMetal004
  recoloured). Ramps: orange-stained planks (`tools/texgen/stain.py`).
- Looks checked offline (numpy renders of both shaders' logic) before the game: not yet in-game.
- Found on the way: the stained plank texture's first import failed (`valid=false`), so the
  ramps didn't load; reimported. Headless Terrain3D empties its texture list once in the tree
  (a dummy-renderer thing, the Jungle does it too): checks read it before.

MSAA / FXAA off). Mid-run the GPU clocked down (baseline 14 -> ~23 ms in the nave: heat or

| Render scale 0.5 | -4.2 | -3.5 |

**After optimising (same day, performance power mode, so the totals aren't comparable with the run
above, only the deltas):** the bevel slope is baked into `<pattern>_slope.png`, so the shader reads
2 textures instead of 5. The art materials now cost **+0.9 ms** (nave) / **+0.3 ms** (overview) over
greybox, down from +2.6 / +1.0. The default render scale went from 0.6 to **0.55**, which saves
1.25–1.3 ms (baseline at 0.6: 14.6 / 14.2 ms). Run only some tests with
`TESTS="greybox materials,render scale 0.55"`.

## Steps

1. **Art test room (the gate).** One real place, the Inferno's BASILICA OF SMOULDER, done fully:
   - a triplanar world shader (`world_triplanar.gdshader`) with vector-style detail: panel lines,
     bevel highlights, bold patterns from textures generated as SVG (`tools/texgen/`), with
     roughness and normal maps from the same shapes;
   - its colour grade, volumetric fog, glow, and GI (SDFGI vs VoxelGI, measured);
   - the lava's glow lighting the hall.

   The owner judges the look and the cost, and we iterate until they approve. Nothing else is
   mass-produced before that.
2. **Material sets per map:** the approved shader applied to every role on the Inferno, then the
   Jungle (teal/green; weathered limestone, dark wood) and the Overworld (warm gold; grass and rock
   terrain, sandstone ruins). Terrain3D gets textured layers to match. The Gym stays greybox (it's a
   measuring tool).
3. **Characters:** a humanoid (made in Blender, or a free rigged pack retargeted to one skeleton)
   with animations: run, slide, jump, fall, wall-slide, dash, slam, frozen, stunned. An AnimationTree
   is driven by the `Character` state and signals. The Torch's fire sits on the model. First person
   still hides your own body (render layer 2).
4. **Environment props** (Blender or packs): braziers, chains, pews, tombs, trees and foliage
   (MultiMesh with visibility ranges), rocks, ruins. Each one swaps in for a greybox box where it
   reads better, keeping the box collision.
5. **VFX:** tag flash, dash trails, ember trails and scorch marks behind the Torch, lava sparks,
   waterfall spray, a retune of the speed overlay.
6. **Performance pass:** occlusion culling between Inferno halls (`OccluderInstance3D`), visibility
   ranges everywhere, per-setting costs re-measured, and new settings for anything optional.

**Done when:** each map has a screenshot the owner says reads as the target style, and the game still
holds its frame budget at the default settings.
